Project

ADR-004 — performance is measured in server round trips, starting with a published Linux baseline

  • **Status**: Accepted (11 November 2026)
  • **Requirements**: PERF-01 (round-trip counts measured and published), and the comparative form of PERF-02 … PERF-07
  • **Evidence**: [`docs/measurements/linux-baseline-9p.md`](../measurements/linux-baseline-9p.md), [`docs/measurements/README.md`](../measurements/README.md)
  • **Code**: [`host/bench/trace.py`](../../host/bench/trace.py) (the QEMU `v9fs_*` parser), [`host/bench/roundtrips.py`](../../host/bench/roundtrips.py) (the table), [`host/bench/report.py`](../../host/bench/report.py)
  • **Proof**: `test_cli_roundtrips`, `test_compare_tables_flags_extra_roundtrips`, `test_cli_roundtrips_rejects_empty_trace` (host pytest suite)

Context

Under QEMU, wall-clock timings depend mostly on the host hardware, the runner load and the cache mode: two honest runs on two machines do not compare, and a judging panel cannot reproduce either. The specification (§6.5) therefore asks for performance to be formulated <em>comparatively</em> and in <strong>server round trips</strong>.

Two options were on the table for the reference:

  1. publish throughput numbers (MiB/s, IOPS) as the headline;
  2. publish the number of protocol requests a POSIX call costs, read from a QEMU trace, and keep wall-clock as a secondary column.

Decision

<strong>Option 2, and publish the reference first.</strong> The unit of account is the round trip — one request sent, one reply received — counted from a real QEMU trace, and the first published table is the <strong>Linux guest baseline</strong> of spec §7.5, not our own driver:

python3 tests/qemu/run_share_check.py --protos 9p --workload 30 \
        --trace baseline.log --workdir /tmp/passerelle-baseline

Recorded on 11 November 2026: QEMU 8.2.2, virtio-9p-pci with security_model=mapped-xattr, a minimal Alpine guest, <strong>30 create/stat/read/unlink cycles</strong>. The trace carried <strong>1 643 events → 822 requests, 822 replies, 63 errors</strong>, and every POSIX operation costs <strong>1.00 round trip per call</strong>.

Two rules make the table trustworthy:

  • the parser **never guesses**: an unparsable line is reported as a parse error rather than dropped silently, and an empty trace exits non-zero;
  • the trace carries **no timestamps**, so latencies are simply *not* reported rather than invented — round trips per call do not depend on them.

Consequences

  • PERF-01 is satisfied by a measurement anyone can reproduce with one command, on any host, and the number does not move when the hardware does.
  • Every later claim (PERF-02 … PERF-07) has a fixed reference to be compared against, including for the two existing clients, so the comparative report is apples to apples.
  • The honest cost: **Passerelle's own column is empty.** Filling it needs a QNX guest, which needs the SDP; until then `docs/measurements/README.md` lists the three missing tables as absent rather than estimated.
  • Counting round trips rewards protocol efficiency (chunking, `msize`, caching policy) and does not hide a slow host; it also cannot show a latency regression, which is why the throughput and priority workloads of `host/bench/loads.py` stay in the harness for the day a guest exists.
